# Paráfrasis de phrasal verbs y contracciones (LIST-104)

> **Fuente:** Spoken English: Flourish Your Language — Carmen, R. (ed.), Chapter 1 "Contractions" (pp. 9–18) y Chapter 9 "Phrasal Verbs" (pp. 118–191).

## 🎯 Objetivo
Decodificar contracciones que suenan igual pero significan cosas distintas y reconocer la paráfrasis de un phrasal verb en las opciones.

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
1. **'s** = *is* o *has*: si sigue un participio (*been, taken*) es **has** (*He's been my friend*); si sigue adjetivo/-ing/sustantivo es **is**.
2. **'d** = *would* o *had*: si sigue verbo base es **would** (*I'd like*); si sigue participio es **had** (*I'd been doing well*).
3. **won't** (= will not) suena distinto de *want*: la vocal de *won't* es como en *go*.
4. **Let's / let's not**: sugerencia (afirmativa / negativa).
5. **would've / could've**: suena "would of" (el libro advierte que *-ouldn't of* es una grafía errónea).
6. Las opciones correctas suelen **parafrasear** phrasal verbs con verbos de una palabra: *call off → cancel*, *put off → postpone*, *look into → investigate*, *figure out → solve/understand*.

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
`'s + participio = has` · `'s + adj/-ing = is` · `'d + V base = would` · `'d + participio = had`

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- "He's been here since last Tuesday = He has been here since last Tuesday" (p. 16)
- "He's asleep = He is asleep" (p. 14)
- "I'd like to meet her = I would like to meet her" / "I'd been doing well until I got hit by a car = I had been doing well…" (p. 18)
- "I won't do it unless you make me do it." (p. 12)
- "Let's keep this just between us." (p. 10)

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ Oír *I'd known* y pensar "I would know" → es pasado perfecto (*I had known*).
- ❌ Escribir *would of* → ✅ *would have* (p. 10).
- ❌ Confundir *won't* con *want*: "I won't go" (no iré) ≠ "I want to go".

## 🔤 Vocabulario clave
| Lo que oyes | Paráfrasis en la opción |
|---|---|
| They called off the game | The game was cancelled |
| We'll look into it | They will investigate the problem |
| I couldn't figure it out | I didn't understand it / couldn't solve it |
| She's been away | She has been away (no está) |
| I'd rather… | I would prefer… |

## 🧪 Práctica estilo TOEFL
```quiz
- tipo: listening
  pregunta: "Woman: Did you talk to Professor Lee about your grade? Man: She's been out of town all week. What does the man mean?"
  opciones: ["The professor is leaving town next week.", "The professor is going to change his grade.", "He hasn't been able to see the professor.", "He was out of town all week."]
  respuesta: C
  explicacion: "'She's been' = she has been (participio 'been'): la profesora ha estado fuera, así que él no ha podido hablar con ella."
- tipo: listening
  pregunta: "Man: Is the department still holding the picnic on Saturday? Woman: No, they've called it off. What does the woman mean?"
  opciones: ["The picnic has been cancelled.", "The picnic has been postponed until Sunday.", "The department called everyone about the picnic.", "The picnic will be held indoors."]
  respuesta: A
  explicacion: "'Call off' = cancelar. La opción 'called everyone' es trampa por la palabra 'called'."
```
