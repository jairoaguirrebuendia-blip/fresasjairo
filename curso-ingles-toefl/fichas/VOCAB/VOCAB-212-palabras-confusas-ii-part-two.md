# Confusing words II (loose/lose … fewer/less) (VOCAB-212)

> **Fuente:** *Building Vocabulary for College* — Smith, R. K., Chapters 18–28 (Part Two), Mastering Confusing Words, pp. 188, 194, 202, 208, 217, 228, 240, 253, 265, 274, 285.

## 🎯 Objetivo
Distinguir pares de palabras que se confunden por forma o sonido y detectar su mal uso en Written Expression.

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
Los capítulos de Part Two continúan con pares confusos. Varios son clásicos del TOEFL *Written Expression*: **fewer** (contables) vs **less** (incontables), **farther** (distancia física) vs **further** (grado), **its** (posesivo) vs **it's** (it is / it has), **who's** vs **whose**, **imply** (sugerir) vs **infer** (deducir).

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
`término` → definición del libro (en inglés) + equivalente en español

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- Because of the threatening weather, there were fewer people at the game than there were yesterday. / Fortunately, we've had less rain this week …
- We decided not to hike any farther because it was getting dark. / The governor said the budget would take further study …
- The car spun off the track when one of its tires came off its rim. / Do you think it's time for us to go?
- I could infer from my dad's scowl that he wasn't happy …

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ *Less students attended* → ✅ *Fewer students* (contable).
- ❌ *The company changed it's logo* → ✅ *its logo* (posesivo).
- ❌ *further from the city* (distancia física, uso estricto) → ✅ *farther*.
- ❌ *I implied from his tone that he was angry* → ✅ *I inferred* (quien escucha infiere).

## 🧪 Práctica estilo TOEFL
```quiz
- tipo: error-id
  pregunta: "There were {A|less} {B|applicants} for the scholarship this year {C|than} there {D|were} last year."
  respuesta: A
  explicacion: "'Applicants' es contable plural → 'fewer', no 'less'."
- tipo: structure
  pregunta: "The dark clouds and sudden wind suggested that a storm was ____."
  opciones: ["eminent", "imminent", "eminently", "immanence"]
  respuesta: B
  explicacion: "'Imminent' = a punto de ocurrir; 'eminent' = distinguido, famoso."
```
