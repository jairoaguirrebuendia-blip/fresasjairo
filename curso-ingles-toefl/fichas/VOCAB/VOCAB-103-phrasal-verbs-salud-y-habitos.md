# Phrasal verbs de salud y hábitos (VOCAB-103)

> **Fuente:** Spoken English: Flourish Your Language — Carmen, R. (ed.), Chapter 9 "Phrasal Verbs" (pp. 121–184) y Chapter 12 "Slangs", *burned out* (pp. 212–213).

## 🎯 Objetivo
Reconocer los phrasal verbs sobre enfermedad, recuperación y cambio de hábitos.

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
Contrastes clave: **give up** (dejar un hábito) vs **take up** (empezar un hábito/pasatiempo); **cut down on** (reducir, sin dejar del todo); **get over** (recuperarse); **pass out / black out** (desmayarse).

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
`give up + V-ing/N` · `take up + N/V-ing` · `cut down on + N` · `get over + N (enfermedad, mala experiencia)`

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- **Give up** = stop doing something that had been a habit: "I gave up taking sugar in tea and coffee to lose weight." (p. 151)
- **Cut down on** = reduce: "Doctors advised her to cut down on the amount of saturated fats in her diet." (p. 139)
- **Take up** = start a new hobby: "He took up squash as he felt he had to lose some weight." (p. 184)
- **Get over** = recover: "It took me ages to get over the bout of flu." (p. 149)
- **Put on** = get fat: "He's put on a lot of weight since he gave up smoking." (p. 168)

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ *give up to smoke* → ✅ *give up smoking* (gerundio).
- ❌ *cut down* (talar) ≠ ✅ *cut down on* (reducir consumo).
- ❌ *take up* solo = levantar: también = ocupar tiempo/espacio y acortar ropa (p. 184).
