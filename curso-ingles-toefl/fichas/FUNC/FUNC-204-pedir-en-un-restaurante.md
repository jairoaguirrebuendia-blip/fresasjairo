# Pedir en un restaurante (FUNC-204)

> **Fuente:** Spoken English: Flourish Your Language — Carmen, R. (ed.), Chapter 6 "Conversation", Ordering a meal (pp. 75–76) y diálogo de restaurante (p. 99).

## 🎯 Objetivo
Reconocer las fórmulas del mesero y del cliente, e inferir el lugar y la relación entre hablantes (pregunta típica: *Where does this conversation probably take place?*).

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
- Pedir: **I'll have…** / **I'd like…** (más cortés que *I want*).
- Mesero: **Would you like something to drink? / Can I get you anything else? / Are you ready to order?**
- **I'm afraid the plaice is off** = ya no hay ese plato (BrE). *I'm afraid* introduce una mala noticia de forma cortés.
- Pagar: **I'd like the check, please** (AmE; el libro aclara *bill* – UK). **Keep the change!** = quédese con el cambio.

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
`I'll have + plato.` · `I'd like + bebida.` · `What do you recommend?` · `I'd like the check, please.`

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- "That sounds good. I'll have that." (p. 75)
- "Yes, I'd like a coke." (p. 76)
- "No thanks. I'd like the check (bill – UK English), please." (p. 76)
- "I'm afraid the plaice is off." — "Oh dear. What do you recommend?" (p. 99)
- "Could we sit by the window?" — "The window tables are all reserved." (p. 99)

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ *I want a coke* (suena brusco) → ✅ *I'd like a coke.*
- ❌ *Bring me the count* → ✅ *Could I have the check, please?*

## 🔤 Vocabulario clave
| Palabra/colocación | Significado | Nota |
|---|---|---|
| today's special(ity) | especialidad del día | AmE *special*, el libro usa *speciality* (BrE) |
| the check / the bill | la cuenta | AmE / BrE |
| Keep the change | quédese con el cambio | propina |
| for starters | de entrada | BrE; AmE *appetizer* |
| is off (a dish) | se acabó | BrE |

## 🧪 Práctica estilo TOEFL
```quiz
- tipo: listening
  pregunta: "Man: I'll have the grilled salmon, please. Woman: I'm afraid we've just run out of it, but the chicken is excellent tonight. What does the woman mean?"
  opciones: ["She is afraid of the salmon.", "The man should order quickly.", "The chicken is too expensive.", "The salmon is not available, so she recommends the chicken."]
  respuesta: D
  explicacion: "'I'm afraid…' anuncia una mala noticia con cortesía (no miedo); 'run out of' = acabarse. Luego sugiere otro plato."
```
