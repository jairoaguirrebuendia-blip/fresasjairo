# Adjective vs adverb; comparativos y superlativos (GRAM-511)

> **Fuente:** English Grammar Master in 30 Days — Gonzales, Xyli C., Day 5 "Linking Verbs" p. 41; Day 17 "Adjectives" pp. 93–98; Day 18 "Adjectives have Comparison" pp. 99–101; Day 19 "Adverbs" pp. 102–105.

## 🎯 Objetivo
Elegir adjetivo o adverbio según la palabra modificada y formar correctamente comparativos y superlativos.

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
- **Adjectives** modify nouns/pronouns: before the noun (*an intelligent man*) or after a **linking verb** (*be, seem, look, feel, become, appear, remain, sound, smell, taste*).
- **Adverbs** modify verbs, adjectives and other adverbs (how, when, where, how often, to what degree). Many end in **-ly** (*careful → carefully*). Adverbs do not modify nouns.
- After a linking verb use an **adjective**, not an adverb (*smells delicious*).
- **Comparative** (two things) + *than*; **superlative** (three or more) + *the*.
- Short words (one syllable, many two-syllable in -y): *-er / -est* (*big–bigger–biggest*, *happy–happier*). Longer words and most *-ly* adverbs: *more/most, less/least* (*more difficult, more carefully*).
- Irregular: *good/well–better–best, bad/badly–worse–worst, little–less–least, many/much–more–most, far–farther/further–farthest/furthest*.
- Absolute adjectives (*perfect, unique, full, final, universal*) normally are not compared.

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
`linking verb + adjective` · `action verb + adverb` · `adj-er / more + adj + than` · `the + adj-est / the most + adj`

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- He is an intelligent man. · She is happy.
- He looks great in his green jacket.
- He writes neatly. · The mango is very sweet.
- My wife was more emotional than her mother.
- Fidie has the sharpest mind in class.

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ *The soup smelled deliciously.* → ✅ *smelled delicious*
- ❌ *She sings beautiful.* → ✅ *She sings beautifully.*
- ❌ *more faster / most biggest* → ✅ *faster / biggest* (doble comparativo)
- ❌ *the highest than* / *taller of the three* → ✅ *higher than* / *the tallest of the three*

## 🔤 Vocabulario clave
| Palabra/colocación | Significado | Nota |
|---|---|---|
| linking verb | verbo copulativo | + adjetivo |
| farther / further | más lejos / adicional, más | distancia vs grado |
| good / well | bueno / bien | adjetivo / adverbio |

```quiz

  pregunta: "The new engine operates ____ than the old one."

  explicacion: "Modifica al verbo 'operates' → adverbio; comparación de dos con 'than' → 'more efficiently'."
- tipo: error-id
  pregunta: "The soup {A|smelled} {B|deliciously}, so the guests {C|eagerly} {D|tasted} it."
  respuesta: B
  explicacion: "'smell' es aquí verbo copulativo: va seguido de adjetivo → 'delicious'."
- tipo: structure
  pregunta: "Mount Everest is ____ mountain in the world."
  opciones: ["higher", "the highest", "the most high", "highest than"]
  respuesta: B
  explicacion: "Comparación con todo el grupo ('in the world') → superlativo 'the highest'."
```

## 🗒️ Notas / Excepciones
- **Errores del libro (no copiar):** define el adverbio como modificador de "a verb, an adjective, or another verb" (p. 102; es *another adverb*); en las tablas: "fast – faster – faster" (p. 105; **fastest**), "slow – lower" (p. 105; **slower**), "out – outer – outset" (p. 100; **outermost**); ejemplos con erratas "Quite Friday" (*quiet*) y "I fell terrible" (*feel*) (pp. 94–95); dice que los adjetivos de una o dos sílabas forman -er/-est (p. 100), pero muchos de dos sílabas usan *more* (*more careful*); llama "infinitive adjectives" a *much, many, little, few* (p. 96; son indefinidos) y pone *all* como adjetivo descriptivo (p. 96).
- Los artículos *a/an/the* aparecen en el libro como un "uso" del adjetivo (p. 95); *an* va ante sonido vocálico (*an hour*), *a* ante sonido consonántico (*a university*).
