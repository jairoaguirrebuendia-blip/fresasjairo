# Verb forms: principal parts y past tense vs past participle (GRAM-505)

> **Fuente:** English Grammar Master in 30 Days — Gonzales, Xyli C., Day 5 "Forms of Verbs / Three Parts of Verbs", pp. 40–43; Day 9 "Regular Verbs / Irregular Verbs / Special Irregular Verbs", pp. 57–61.

## 🎯 Objetivo
Usar la forma correcta del verbo: pasado simple solo, y participio pasado después de *have/has/had* o *be* (pasiva).

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
- Verb forms: **base** (*explain, eat*), **-s form** (*explains*), **-ing / present participle** (*explaining*), **past** (*explained, ate*), **past participle** (*explained, eaten*).
- **Regular verbs** add *-d/-ed*; past = past participle (*act–acted–acted*). Spelling: doble consonante en *equip–equipped, infer–inferred, kidnap–kidnapped*.
- **Irregular verbs** change form (*begin–began–begun, choose–chose–chosen, tear–tore–torn*); some never change (*cut, hit, put, let, set, quit, hurt, bet*).
- The **past participle** goes after *have/has/had* (perfect tenses) and after *be* (passive). The simple past goes alone.
- In a verb phrase the last verb is the main verb; *will/shall/do/does/did* + **base form** (no -s, no -ed, no -ing).

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
`S + past` · `have/has/had + past participle` · `do/does/did/modal + base form`

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- He ate bread. → He has eaten bread.
- The child played basketball. → The child had played basketball with me.
- begin–began–begun · drive–drove–driven · ring–rang–rung · sink–sank–sunk
- Errores que señala el libro: *done* por *did*, *seen* por *saw*, *went* por *gone*, *swam* por *swum*, *began* por *begun*.

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ *She has went home.* → ✅ *She has gone home.*
- ❌ *The bell had rang.* → ✅ *The bell had rung.*
- ❌ *I seen it yesterday.* → ✅ *I saw it yesterday.*
- ❌ *Did she wrote?* → ✅ *Did she write?* (did + base)

## 🔤 Vocabulario clave
| Palabra/colocación | Significado | Nota |
|---|---|---|
| principal parts | partes principales | base – past – past participle |
| auxiliary / helping verb | verbo auxiliar | have, be, do, modales |
| arise–arose–arisen | surgir | frecuente en textos académicos |

## 🧪 Práctica estilo TOEFL
```quiz
- tipo: structure
  pregunta: "By the end of the semester, the students had ____ all the required novels."
  opciones: ["read", "readed", "reading", "to read"]
  respuesta: A
  explicacion: "had + participio pasado; el participio de 'read' es 'read' (irregular, se pronuncia /red/)."
- tipo: error-id
  pregunta: "The bell {A|had} already {B|rang} {C|when} the professor {D|arrived}."
  respuesta: B
  explicacion: "Tras 'had' va participio: ring–rang–rung → 'had rung'."
- tipo: structure
  pregunta: "The engineer ____ the report before the meeting started."
  opciones: ["has wrote", "written", "wrote", "had write"]
  respuesta: C
  explicacion: "Pasado simple sin auxiliar: 'wrote'. 'has wrote' y 'had write' combinan mal auxiliar y forma; 'written' solo necesita auxiliar."
```

## 🗒️ Notas / Excepciones
- **Errores del libro (no copiar):** "awake – awoke – awaken" (p. 59; participio correcto **awoken**); "Hear" aparece en la lista de verbos regulares (p. 58; es irregular: *hear–heard–heard*); en la tabla de participios "Bleeding – blend" (p. 138; correcto **bled**), "having born" (p. 138; en voz activa **having borne**), "having awoke" y "forgotten, forgot" como participio perfecto (correcto *having awoken*, *having forgotten*).
- *got/gotten*: *gotten* es la forma americana habitual; ambas aparecen en el libro.
