# Nouns with count and non-count meanings; a piece of / a cup of (GRAM-312)

> **Fuente:** *Focus on Grammar 5*, 5th ed. — autor no consta en el documento, Unit 10 — Count and Non-Count Nouns (Part 5), Grammar Notes 4–5 — Count and Non-Count Meanings of Nouns; Making Non-Count Nouns Countable, pp. 165–166.

## 🎯 Objetivo
Reconocer cuándo un sustantivo cambia de significado al usarse como contable y cómo volver contable un incontable.

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
Muchos sustantivos tienen sentido incontable (masa) y contable (ejemplo individual o 'tipo/variedad de'): *history / a history*, *exercise / an exercise*, *tea / teas*. No todos los incontables pueden volverse contables (*traffic, fun*). Para contar un incontable se usa una frase de forma (*a drop of rain*), límite (*a piece of advice*) o recipiente (*a cup of coffee*), que además es más precisa.

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
`a/an + noun = one example / a kind of | a piece/drop/grain/cup/can/loaf + of + non-count noun`

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- Film is a major art form. / I've seen a lot of interesting **films** lately. (individual movies)
- I used to be a professor of **history**. / I read **a history** of the Civil War. (a single book)
- That shop sells many different **teas**. (kinds of tea)
- We were stuck in **traffic** for hours. NOT *…in a traffic…*
- May I give you **a piece of advice**? (more precise)

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ *We had a fun yesterday.* → ✅ *We had fun yesterday.*
- ❌ *He gave me two advices.* → ✅ *two pieces of advice*
- ❌ *I drink a can of sodas with every meal.* → ✅ *a can of soda*
- ❌ *We were stuck in a traffic.* → ✅ *in traffic*
