# Gerunds & infinitives (GRAM-518)

> **Fuente:** English Grammar Master in 30 Days — Gonzales, Xyli C., Chapter V (Day 27) "Gerunds / Infinitives / Split Infinitives", pp. 147–150.

## 🎯 Objetivo
Usar gerundio o infinitivo según la función y la palabra que los precede.

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
- A **gerund** (-ing) works as a **noun**: subject, direct object, object of a preposition.
- After a **preposition** always use the gerund (*interested in visiting*, *objects to my joining*).
- A possessive before a gerund is the formal choice (*my joining*).
- An **infinitive** (*to + base*) works as a noun (*To sing was…*), an adjective (*no desire to live*) or an adverb (*sorry to learn*).
- *to* + verb = infinitive marker; *to* + noun/-ing = preposition (*look forward to meeting*).
- **Bare infinitive** (without *to*) after *let, make, see, watch, hear, feel* (and optionally *help*): *We saw him enter*.
- **Split infinitive** (*to + adverb + verb*): in formal writing prefer *not to sell*, *to desist immediately*.

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
`preposition + V-ing` · `let/make/see/watch/hear + object + base form` · `It is + adj + to V` · `noun + to V`

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- Boxing is Manny Pacquiao's sport. (gerund subject)
- Correct: The Doctor objects to my joining the race.
- To sing was Laila's ambition. · Her ambition was to sing. · She loves to sing.
- In his eyes there was no desire to live.
- We saw him enter the room. · Her mother expected him not to sell the lot.

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ *interested in visit* → ✅ *interested in visiting*
- ❌ *made the employees to stay* → ✅ *made the employees stay*
- ❌ *Swim is good exercise.* → ✅ *Swimming is good exercise.*
- ❌ *I look forward to meet you.* → ✅ *to meeting you*

## 🔤 Vocabulario clave
| Palabra/colocación | Significado | Nota |
|---|---|---|
| object to + -ing | oponerse a | *to* es preposición |
| look forward to + -ing | esperar con ganas | |
| avoid / enjoy / finish + -ing | evitar / disfrutar / terminar | no lo trata el libro |
| want / decide / plan + to V | querer / decidir / planear | no lo trata el libro |

## 🗒️ Notas / Excepciones
- **Errores del libro (no copiar):** "In contrast to a principle" (p. 147; quiere decir *participle*); incluye *ask* entre los verbos con infinitivo sin *to*: "I ask her (to) help" (p. 149; con *ask* el *to* es obligatorio: *I ask her to help*) y lista *bid, need, please, bear, dare* sin matices; "The necessity to call a police" (p. 149; *the police*); presenta "The ladies were convinced to dance" como infinitivo adverbial que modifica a *convinced* (análisis discutible).
- El libro no trata las listas de verbos + gerundio / + infinitivo; conviene completarlas con otra fuente.
