# Participial adjectives: -ing (boring) vs. -ed (bored) (GRAM-321)

> **Fuente:** *Focus on Grammar 5*, 5th ed. — autor no consta en el documento, Unit 13 — Modification of Nouns (Part 5), Grammar Notes 2 — Adjective Modifiers (present and past participles), p. 215.

## 🎯 Objetivo
Distinguir el adjetivo en -ing (lo que causa el sentimiento) del adjetivo en -ed/-en (quien lo experimenta).

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
El participio presente (-ing) describe a alguien o algo que causa un sentimiento; el participio pasado (-ed/-en) describe a quien experimenta el sentimiento.

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
`thing/person that causes the feeling + V-ing | person who feels it + V-ed/-en`

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- It was a **boring** movie. (The movie caused the viewers to be bored.)
- We heard some **shocking** news today.
- The **bored** viewers left the movie.
- There were a lot of **shocked** people who heard the news.

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ *I'm very boring in this class.* (quiere decir 'me aburro') → ✅ *I'm very bored.*
- ❌ *It was an excited game.* → ✅ *an exciting game*
- ❌ *The results were surprised.* → ✅ *surprising*
- ❌ *She gave an interested lecture.* → ✅ *an interesting lecture*

## 🔤 Vocabulario clave
| Palabra/colocación | Significado | Nota |
|---|---|---|
| boring / bored | aburrido (que aburre) / aburrido (que se aburre) |  |
| exhausting / exhausted | agotador / agotado |  |
| frightening / frightened | aterrador / asustado |  |
