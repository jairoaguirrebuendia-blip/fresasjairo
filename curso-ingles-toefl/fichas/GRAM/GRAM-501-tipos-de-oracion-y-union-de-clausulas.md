# Sentence structure: simple, compound, complex; comma splice y run-on (GRAM-501)

> **Fuente:** English Grammar Master in 30 Days — Gonzales, Xyli C., Chapter I (Day 1) "Four Basic Sentence Structures", pp. 14–17; Chapter IV (Day 24) "Using Comma and Semicolon", pp. 130–134.

## 🎯 Objetivo
Clasificar oraciones por sus cláusulas y unir dos cláusulas independientes correctamente (sin *comma splice* ni *run-on*).

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
- **Simple**: one independent clause (may have a compound subject or predicate).
- **Compound**: two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (*for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so* = FANBOYS) or by a semicolon.
- **Complex**: one independent clause + one or more dependent clauses (introduced by *because, although, when, while, if…*).
- **Compound-complex**: two or more independent clauses + at least one dependent clause.
- **Comma fault (comma splice)**: two independent clauses joined only by a comma. **Run-on**: two independent clauses with no punctuation and no connector.
- Fixes: semicolon; comma + FANBOYS; a subordinator; or two separate sentences.
- **Transitional words** (*however, therefore, moreover, as a result, meanwhile…*) do not join clauses by themselves: use `; transition,`.

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
`IC, FANBOYS IC` · `IC; IC` · `IC; however, IC` · `DC, IC` · `IC DC`
(IC = independent clause, DC = dependent clause)

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- Joy waited for the train, but the train was late. (compound)
- Eve wrote a book; her husband published it. (semicolon)
- ❌ *President Juan campaigned hard in the cities, he also visited the remote towns.* (comma fault)
- We paid them as scheduled; as a result, we were given discounts.
- While she waited at the train station, Joy realized that the train was late. (complex / periodic)

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ *The test was hard, however we passed.* → ✅ *The test was hard; however, we passed.*
- ❌ *Many birds migrate, they return in spring.* → ✅ *Many birds migrate, and they return in spring.*
- ❌ *Although it rained, but we played.* → ✅ *Although it rained, we played.* (no se combinan subordinante y coordinante para la misma relación)

## 🔤 Vocabulario clave
| Palabra/colocación | Significado | Nota |
|---|---|---|
| FANBOYS | for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so | coordinantes; van tras coma |
| comma splice / comma fault | unión indebida con coma | error típico |
| however / therefore / moreover | sin embargo / por lo tanto / además | adverbios conjuntivos: `; …,` |

## 🧪 Práctica estilo TOEFL
```quiz
- tipo: structure
  pregunta: "The library closes at noon on Saturdays; ____, students should plan to study elsewhere."
  opciones: ["because", "therefore", "although", "so that"]
  respuesta: B
  explicacion: "Tras punto y coma y antes de coma va un adverbio conjuntivo (therefore). 'because', 'although' y 'so that' son subordinantes y no encajan en ese patrón."
- tipo: error-id
  pregunta: "Many birds migrate south in winter{A|, they} return {B|in} the spring {C|when} the weather {D|becomes} warmer."
  respuesta: A
  explicacion: "Comma splice: dos cláusulas independientes unidas solo por coma. Corrección: ', and they' o '; they'."
- tipo: structure
  pregunta: "____ the experiment failed twice, the team refused to give up."
  opciones: ["But", "Although", "However", "Despite"]
  respuesta: B
  explicacion: "Se necesita un subordinante seguido de cláusula (S + V). 'However' no subordina y 'Despite' va con sustantivo o -ing."
```

## 🗒️ Notas / Excepciones
- **Errores del libro (no copiar):** en la lista FANBOYS aparece "Not" en lugar de **Nor** (p. 15); afirma que la oración *compound* expresa relaciones más claras que la *complex* (p. 16), cuando es al revés; el ejemplo "…late afternoons; but, I managed to cope" (p. 132) lleva una coma incorrecta tras *but*; la lista de transiciones (pp. 133–134) incluye "passively" y "yet", que no son adverbios conjuntivos de ese tipo.
- Con cláusulas muy cortas se puede omitir la coma antes de *and/but*.
