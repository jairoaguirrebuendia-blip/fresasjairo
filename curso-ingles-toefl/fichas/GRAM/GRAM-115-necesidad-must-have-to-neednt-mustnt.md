# Necesidad: must, have to, needn't, mustn't (GRAM-115)

> **Fuente:** Oxford Guide to English Grammar — Eastwood, J., Chapter 12, §92, pp. 115–118.

## 🎯 Objetivo
Distinguir obligación (*must/have to*), ausencia de obligación (*needn't/don't have to*) y prohibición (*mustn't*).

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
- **must**: el hablante siente la necesidad / da la orden. **have to**: necesidad externa (regla, situación).
- *must* no tiene pasado ni otras formas → **had to, have had to, will have to, having to**.
- *have to* usa **do** en negativas y preguntas: *We don't have to pay.*
- **needn't / don't have to / don't need to** = no es necesario. **mustn't** = está prohibido (sentidos opuestos).
- **needn't have + V3** = se hizo pero no era necesario. **didn't need to** = normalmente no se hizo.
- Formales: *be obliged to, be required to*.

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
`must / have to + V1` · `had to + V1` (pasado) · `needn't have + V3`

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- You must buy your ticket before starting your journey. / I have to buy the ticket before I get on the train.
- I had to pay £15 for this railcard last week.
- I needn't run. I've got plenty of time. / I mustn't run. I've got a weak heart.
- We needn't have made these sandwiches. No one's eaten any.
- Students must not/may not use dictionaries in the examination.

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ *Yesterday we must stay late.* → ✅ *Yesterday we **had to** stay late.*
- ❌ *You mustn't come if you're busy* (= no es necesario) → ✅ *You **don't have to** come…*
- ❌ *Does the winner has to make a speech?* → ✅ *Does the winner **have to**…?*

## 🔤 Vocabulario clave
| Palabra/colocación | Significado | Nota |
|---|---|---|
| be required to | estar obligado | formal |
| there's no need to | no hace falta | need como sustantivo |
