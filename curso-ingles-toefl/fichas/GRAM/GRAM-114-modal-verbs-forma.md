# Modal verbs: forma (GRAM-114)

> **Fuente:** Oxford Guide to English Grammar — Eastwood, J., Chapter 12, §91, pp. 113–115.

## 🎯 Objetivo
Detectar errores de forma con modales (*can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would, ought to*).

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
- El modal es siempre la **primera palabra** del verbo, **nunca cambia** (sin -s, -ing, -ed) y va seguido de **base form** sin *to* (excepto *ought to*).
- Negativa con *not* directamente; pregunta invirtiendo modal y sujeto; **nunca con do**.
- No tiene tiempo: se refiere a presente o futuro. Para el pasado: *had to, was able to* o **modal + have + V3**.
- Puede combinarse con perfect, continuous y passive: *may have shown, may be showing, may be shown, must have been stolen*.
- **No** hay dos modales seguidos (*will must*); se usa *will have to, might be able to*.
- Los modales no tienen infinitivo ni -ing: *to be allowed to*, *being able to*.

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
`modal + V1` · `modal + have + V3` · `modal + be + V-ing` · `modal + be + V3`

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- It will be windy. You should look after your money.
- NOT *How do I should organize my work?*
- We had to do a test yesterday. NOT *We must do a test yesterday.*
- The car **must have been stolen**.
- You **will have to** hurry. NOT *You will must hurry.*

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ *She cans swim.* / *He musts leave.* → ✅ *She **can** swim.*
- ❌ *Scientists should to test…* → ✅ *Scientists **should test**…*
- ❌ *Being can see is important.* → ✅ ***Being able to** see…*
- ❌ *You will can finish.* → ✅ *You **will be able to** finish.*

## 🔤 Vocabulario clave
| Palabra/colocación | Significado | Nota |
|---|---|---|
| have to / be able to / be allowed to | tener que / poder / tener permiso | semi-modales; tienen todas las formas |
| ought to | debería | único modal con to |
