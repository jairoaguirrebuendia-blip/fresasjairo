# -ing and -ed adjectives; adjective + to/that (GRAM-220)

> **Fuente:** Intermediate English Grammar and Practice — Unit 33 "Adjective + 'to' or 'that'", p. 36; Unit 34 "'-ing' and '-ed' adjectives", p. 37.

## 🎯 Objetivo
Distinguir *boring/bored, interesting/interested* y completar adjetivos con *to*-infinitivo o *that*-clause.

## 📘 Regla / Concepto
**-ing / -ed (Unit 34):**
- *-ing* describe el **efecto** que algo produce: *a surprising number* (el número sorprende).
- *-ed* describe lo que **siente** una persona (sentido pasivo): *a frightened person* (alguien fue asustado).
- Algunos *-ing* describen un proceso continuo: *ageing, increasing, existing, remaining*.
- Participios irregulares también funcionan como adjetivo: *a broken wing*.
- Algunos *-ed* van casi solo tras copulativo + preposición/to/that: *pleased with, interested in, scared that...*

**Adjetivo + to / that (Unit 33):**
- Adjetivos de sentimiento (*afraid, glad, happy, proud, sorry, surprised*): mismo sujeto → *to*-infinitivo; sujeto distinto → **that**-clause.
- Siempre con *to*-inf.: *able, likely, unlikely, willing, unwilling, ready, prepared, bound, due, liable*.
- Opinión con *to*-inf.: *easy, difficult, impossible, right, wrong* (*She had been easy to deceive*).
- Opinión con *that*: *essential, important, obvious, true, extraordinary*.
- *It + be + adj + of/for + persona + to-inf.*: *It was good of John to help me. It was difficult for her to find a job.*

## 🧩 Estructura / Fórmula
- `thing + is + V-ing adj` · `person + feels/is + V-ed adj (+ prep)`
- `adj + to-infinitive` (same subject) · `adj + that + clause` (different subject)
- `It is + adj + for + person + to-infinitive`

## 💬 Ejemplos (del texto)
- This is the most terrifying tale ever written.
- A bored student complained to his teacher.
- I was happy to see them again. / He was happy that they were coming.
- They were not likely to forget it.
- It was difficult for her to find a job.

## ⚠️ Errores comunes (clave TOEFL)
- ❌ *I am very interesting in history.* → ✅ *I am very **interested** in history.*
- ❌ *The lecture was bored.* → ✅ *The lecture was **boring**.*
- ❌ *He is able of swimming.* → ✅ *He is able **to swim**.*
- ❌ *It was difficult to her to find a job.* → ✅ *...**for** her to find...*
